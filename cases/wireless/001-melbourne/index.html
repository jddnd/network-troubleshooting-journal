RF analysis: unusually strong overlap

All three 5 GHz radios were operating at 17 dBm on separate 20 MHz channels. Auto-RF showed the APs hearing one another extremely strongly.

Working interpretationThe office had many strong roam candidates. FT makes a roam cheaper, but it does not fix a client that decides to roam too often.

Current conclusion

Probable, not yet confirmed: the strongest current explanation is excessive client roaming associated with very strong 5 GHz RF overlap, with user impact amplified on clients that negotiate non-FT roaming and therefore perform full EAP-TLS during transitions.

Engineering lessons

01
Healthy RADIUS does not prove healthy roaming.

An RF/client failure can occur before a RADIUS request is generated.

02
Authentication events are not disconnect counters.

ISE can show repeated successful events while the real question is how and why the client moved.

03
FT reduces roam cost, not roam frequency.

A 20–40 ms transition is better than a full EAP exchange, but excessive roaming remains excessive.

04
A/B testing can kill attractive hypotheses quickly.

A matched client proved that the driver and broad AX211 hardware combination could support FT.

05
AP-to-AP RSSI is useful design evidence.

Very strong NDP neighbor measurements made cell overlap measurable rather than subjective.

06
Change one variable and verify convergence.

The 17 → 14 dBm test remained attributable because 6 GHz, channels, FT and identity policy were held constant.
